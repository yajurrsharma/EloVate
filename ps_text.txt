Mind Over Money
OBJECTIVE
Designed for first-time investors, this accessible platform demystifies complex
financial jargon, charts, and metrics into easily digestible insights. By cutting through
social media hype, it empowers young users to build genuine financial literacy and
make confident, informed investment decisions.

TEAM SIZE:
4 members : (Product Manager , Designer , 2 Developers)

FOCUS AREAS:
1. Leverage language models to process financial documents, balance sheets and
market terminology.
2. Transform high-density financial APIs and traditional market charts into lowfriction, intuitive graphical interfaces.
3. Develop algorithms that evaluate an asset's risk and match it to a user's
profile, abstracting heavy market numbers into clear indicators.
4. Build data-driven logic flows and recommendation engines that analyze market
fundamentals to counter impulsive, trend-based user behavior.

MINIMUM PRODUCT SCOPE :
1.A beginner-friendly dashboard showcasing simplified asset profiles (stocks,
funds, or crypto).
2.A built-in feature translating complex terms and metrics into plain English.
3.A visual risk-indicator tool showing clear safety and volatility levels for each
asset.
4.A simulation or guided decision-flow helping users evaluate investments
without hype.

THE PROBLEM STATEMENT

Campus Recruitment
OBJECTIVE
This transparent platform ensures integrity in online assessments and virtual
interviews by verifying candidate skills and authentic project experience.
Additionally, it empowers hackathon participants to validate their abilities and
connect with ideal teammates.

TEAM SIZE:
4 members : (Product Manager , Designer , 2 Developers)

FOCUS AREAS:
1.Verify claimed technical skills and project experience through
assessments and external technical platforms.
2.Test a participant's technical skill stack before they are added to a
hackathon team.
3.Enable participants to find and form teams based on verified and
complementary skills.

MINIMUM PRODUCT SCOPE :
1. A candidate profile system where claimed skills and experiences are
backed by verifiable proof.
2. An online assessment system to validate a participant's actual
technical skills.
3. A skill-based team discovery and formation system for hackathons.
4. A mechanism for teams to test and verify a potential member's skills
before adding them.
5. A dashboard showing verified skills, assessment results, and team
participation.

DO’S AND DONT’S
DO’S OF THIS HACK
Focus on MVP – Prioritise core features (user registration, course
creation, basic payments).
Collaborate effectively – Clear role assignments and communication.
Use simple, effective tech – Stick to familiar tools and frameworks.
Ensure trust and security – Implement basic user verification (e.g.,
ratings, reviews).
Test as you go – Regularly test features for functionality.
Prepare for the pitch – Be clear and concise in your presentation.
Stay adaptable – Adjust your plan based on feedback and challenges.
Follow accessibility standards – Ensure a user-friendly, accessible
and responsive interface.

DONT’S OF THIS HACK
Don't overcomplicate features – Focus on essential features only.
Don't neglect UI/UX – Avoid confusing or complex interfaces.
Don't ignore testing – Test features regularly, don't leave it all to the
end.
Don't obsess over perfection – Aim for a working solution, not
perfection.
Don't overuse unnecessary tech – Avoid adding complex technologies
unless crucial.
Don't ignore feedback – Take mentor advice seriously and adjust
accordingly.
Don't miss the final demo – Make sure to rehearse and demo
smoothly.

GENERAL RULES
TIMEFRAME
The hackathon lasts for 18 hours.
Teams must submit their required products or presentation by
the designated deadlines.
No extensions will be granted; all teams must strictly adhere to
the timeline

PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS
The goal is to build a working prototype showcasing the core
features of the solution.
The platform must be functional - it should allow judges and
mentors to interact with your submissions.
Features like user registration, course creation, payment
systems must be working.

MEMBER AND SUPPORT
Teams will have first 2 rounds of mentorship feedback
during the hackathon.
Mentors will provide guidance, but teams are expected to
work independently to solve problems.
Teams should actively engage with mentors during their
designated rounds.
Final round will include judging from our panel.

GENERAL RULES
TECHNOLOGY STACK
You may use any technology or tools of your choice.
While technologies like blockchain and AI can be used, they are
optional. Only incorporate them if they add significant value to
your solution.
Avoid overcomplicating the tech stack; choose tools that your
team is comfortable with and that align with your goals.

CODE OF CONDUCT
Respect and professionalism must be maintained throughout
the hackathon.
Any form of plagiarism, harassment, or inappropriate
behavior will result in disqualification.
Teams must ensure that the code and design are original, or
if third-party resources are used, they must be properly
credited.

INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY:
All code, designs, and assets created during the hackathon
remain the property of the team.
Teams are free to continue developing the project after the
hackathon ends.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Q: Can we use any technology stack?
A: Yes, you are free to choose any technologies that
suit your team’s expertise. Blockchain, AI, and other
advanced technologies are optional.

working by the deadline?
A: Your platform must include the core features

function and demonstrate the main functionality.

Q: Will the judges' decision be final?
A: Yes, the judges' decision is final. If any disputes arise, the
hackathon organizers will intervene to resolve them
transparently.

CONFLICT RESOLUTION

1.Mentor Disagreements:
Case: A team receives conflicting advice or feedback from
mentors.
Resolution: Teams should consider both perspectives and
decide what fits best within their project's scope. If needed,
organizers will intervene to provide clarity.

2.Technical Issues During Demo:
Case: A team faces technical difficulties during the final
presentation or demo.
Resolution: Teams will be given a few minutes to explain and
showcase the functional aspects of their project. Judges will
focus on what is working.

3.Late Submission:
Case: A team submits after the official deadline.
Resolution: Late submissions will not be accepted. No
exceptions will be made to the submission time.

CONFLICT RESOLUTION

4.Feature Discrepancies:
Case: A team believes their submission was judged unfairly
due to missing features.
Resolution: Judges' decision is final. If the core features
(registration, payment, etc.) are met, the team will be
evaluated based on completeness and functionality.

5.Code/Design Ownership Disputes:
Case: Teams have concerns over code/design ownership or
plagiarism allegations.
Resolution: Teams must ensure all code and designs are
original or properly credited. Any issues should be reported
to organizers for investigation. Plagiarism will result in
disqualification.

6.Judging Dispute:
Case: A team disagrees with their final score or evaluation.
Resolution: Judges' decision is final and binding. The
organizers will ensure the process is transparent but will
not change results once they are announced.
